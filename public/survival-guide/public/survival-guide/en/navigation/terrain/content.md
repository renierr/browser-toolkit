# Navigating by Terrain

When you don't have a compass or GPS, the landscape itself is your map. Understanding how water flows and how mountains form can guide you.

## 1. Handrails and Catch Features

- **Handrails**: Linear features you can follow parallel to your path. E.g., a river, a ridgeline, a powerline, or a dirt road. If you stay beside the river, you won't get lost.
- **Catch Features (Backstops)**: Massive, unmistakable landmarks (like a lake, highway, or mountain base) that let you know you've gone too far. "If I hit the highway, I know I've missed the trail."

## 2. Reading Topography

- **Ridgelines**: Following the high ground (ridgelines) offers easier walking with less dense vegetation and provides excellent vantage points for spotting civilization.
- **Valleys and Draws**: These always lead downward. While they often contain water, in rugged terrain they can quickly turn into impassable cliffs or waterfalls. 

## 3. The Watershed Rule

Water flows along the path of least resistance. 
- Small streams feed into larger creeks.
- Creeks feed into rivers.
- Rivers reliably lead to human settlements, towns, and roads.

:::tip
Always follow water downstream if you want to find civilization. If the terrain directly adjacent to the river is too rugged, move slightly up the slope to traverse parallel to the water.
:::

## 4. Keeping a Straight Line

Humans naturally walk in circles when lacking a focal point.
1. Pick a highly visible landmark far in the distance directly on your heading.
2. Walk exactly toward it.
3. Before reaching it, pick the *next* landmark on the same heading.

:::warning
Avoid constantly looking at your feet. Check your distant landmark every 50 steps to stop yourself from veering off course.
:::
