# Packing a Bug-Out Bag

A well-packed backpack can mean the difference between traveling 20 miles comfortably or breaking down after 5 miles with severe back pain.

## Principles of Packing

The ABCs of packing a backpack:
- **A**ccessibility
- **B**alance
- **C**ompression

## Weight Distribution

Proper weight distribution is critical for balance and comfort.

1. **Bottom Zone**: Bulky, light items you won't need until camp.
   - Sleeping bag
   - Extra clothing

2. **Core Zone (Close to Spine)**: The heaviest items. Keep them centered and close to your back.
   - Water bladders/bottles
   - Food and rations
   - Tent poles/heavy tools

3. **Top Zone**: Medium weight items or items needed quickly on the trail.
   - First aid kit
   - Rain gear
   - Water filter

4. **Pockets & Pouches**: Small, frequently used items.
   - Map and compass
   - Snacks
   - Knife
   - Flashlight

:::tip
Always pack your heavy items close to your center of gravity (mid-back) to prevent the backpack from pulling you backward.
:::

## Organization

- **Use Dry Bags**: Sort your gear into colored dry bags. This modular approach keeps gear dry and organized (e.g., Red for First Aid, Blue for Clothing, Green for Food).
- **Eliminate Dead Space**: Stuff small items into pots or shoes.
- **Keep it Tight**: Use compression straps to prevent gear from shifting while you walk.

:::warning
Avoid strapping things to the outside of your pack! They swing freely, throw off your balance, and can snag on branches.
:::

## Checklist
- [ ] Sleeping gear at the bottom
- [ ] Heavy items (water, food) against the spine
- [ ] Rain gear easily accessible at the top
- [ ] First aid kit marked and easily reachable
- [ ] Pack compressed and fitted to your body
