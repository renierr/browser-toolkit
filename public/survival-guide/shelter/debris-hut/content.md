# Debris Hut

A debris hut uses natural materials to create an insulated shelter. Properly built, it can be warmer inside than outside, even in freezing conditions.

## Choosing the Site

1. **Find level ground** - Avoid low areas that collect water
2. **Look for natural windbreaks** - Hills, rocks, or dense vegetation
3. **Check for hazards** - Dead branches above, anthills, unstable ground
4. **Near resources** - Wood for fire, water source, and materials

## Materials Needed

- **Ridgepole**: Strong pole, 9-12 feet long
- **Ribs**: Poles or branches, 3-4 feet long (many needed)
- **Leaves/debris**: For insulation - huge pile needed
- **Ground cover**: Pine boughs, grass, leaves

## Building Steps

### 1. Create the Ridge

1. Find two forked sticks for the ends
2. Place forked sticks about 7 feet apart
3. Place ridgepole in the forks
4. Angle the ridgepole about 30 degrees

### 2. Add Ribs

1. Place sticks along both sides of ridgepole
2. Space them about 6 inches apart
3. Push into ground to anchor
4. Should create a rib-cage structure

### 3. Build the Lattice

1. Weave smaller branches through ribs
2. Creates a matrix to hold debris
3. Goes from ground level up to ridge

### 4. Add Insulation (Massive Amount!)

1. Layer debris at least 2-3 feet thick
2. Start from bottom, work up
3. Use: leaves, pine needles, grass
4. More is better - you can't over-insulate

### 5. Create the Bed

1. Fill inside with 2+ feet of dry debris
2. Add pine boughs for sleeping surface
3. The more layers, the warmer

### 6. Build a Fire Reflector

1. Build a wall of logs or rocks behind the fire
2. Reflects heat back into shelter
3. Keep fire 4-6 feet from shelter wall

## Tips

- The debris layer is the insulation - not the branches
- You need a HUGE pile of debris - armful after armful
- Make the entrance small to retain heat
- Ventilate slightly to prevent CO2 buildup
- Build smaller than you think - easier to heat

## Sizes

- **One person**: About 3 feet wide, 7 feet long
- **Two people**: About 5 feet wide, 7 feet long
- Taller is not better - harder to heat
