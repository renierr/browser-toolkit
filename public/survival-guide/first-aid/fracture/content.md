# Treating Fractures

A fracture is a broken bone. Without professional medical care, proper stabilization is crucial to prevent further injury.

## Signs of Fracture

- Severe pain that worsens with movement
- Swelling and bruising
- Deformity or odd angle of limb
- Inability to move or use the limb
- Bone visible through skin (compound fracture)

## Immediate Actions

1. **Do not move** the injured area unnecessarily
2. **Call for help** if possible
3. **Control any bleeding** (see bleeding guide)
4. **Keep victim still** until splint is ready

## Making a Splint

### Materials

- Rigid material: Boards, branches, rolled newspapers
- Padding: Cloth, moss, grass
- Binding: Shoelaces, strips of cloth, vines

### Steps

1. **Find rigid material** long enough to extend past the joint above and below the break

2. **Pad the splint** with soft material

3. **Immobilize the joint** above and below the fracture

4. **Secure firmly** but not too tight - check circulation

5. **Check circulation** regularly (pulse, warmth, color)

## Specific Fractures

### Arm Fracture

- Splint from elbow to fingers
- Create a sling using cloth around the neck
- Keep arm at about 90 degrees

### Leg Fracture

- Splint the entire leg
- Tie the good leg to the injured leg as a makeshift splint
- Do not try to straighten the leg

### Finger/Toe Fracture

- Tape injured finger/toe to adjacent one
- Use padding between digits

## Open Fracture (Bone Through Skin)

1. **Do not push bone back in**
2. **Cover wound** with clean cloth
3. **Control bleeding** around the wound
4. **Splint as normal**
5. **Seek professional help immediately**

## Signs of Complications

- Numbness or tingling
- Blue or pale fingers/toes
- Cold extremity below splint
- Severe pain despite immobilization

**IMPORTANT**: These are emergency measures. Seek professional medical care as soon as possible.
