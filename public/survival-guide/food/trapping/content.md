# Simple Traps

Trapping is an effective way to catch small game for food. Set multiple traps to increase chances.

## Important Principles

1. **Set many traps**: One trap rarely works
2. **Check regularly**: Twice daily if possible
3. **Be patient**: May take days
4. **Practice**: Practice setting traps before you need them

## Simple Snare

### Materials

- Wire (coat hanger, guitar string) or natural cord
- Sticks for anchor

### Steps

1. Find animal trail
2. Create small loop with wire (small enough that animal can't push through)
3. Position loop at animal's head height
4. Anchor to strong stick or log
5. Camouflage with natural materials
6. Bait with food if desired

## Figure-4 Deadfall

### Materials

- 3 roughly equal sticks
- Small bait

### Steps

1. Carve notches in two sticks to interlock
2. Set up as shown (search for diagram)
3. Balance trigger stick with bait
4. Animal touches bait, trigger releases heavy weight

## Scrape Pole

### Purpose

Catches birds

### Steps

1. Find or make a long pole
2. Smooth the end
3. Coat with sticky substance (pine sap, mud that dries hard)
4. Prop horizontally in trees
5. Birds land and get stuck

## Where to Set Traps

- **Animal trails**: Look for tracks, droppings, worn paths
- **Near water**: Animals come to drink
- **Feeding areas**: Nut trees, berry bushes
- **Cover**: Places where animals feel safe

## Types of Animals to Target

- **Rabbits**: Most common, widespread
- **Squirrels**: Tree-dwelling, active during day
- **Chipmunks**: Smaller, easier to catch
- **Birds**: Various methods

## Bait Ideas

- Fruits and berries
- Nuts and seeds
- Vegetables
- Meat scraps
- Fresh greens

## Legal Note

In survival situations, trapping for food is acceptable. In normal circumstances, check local regulations. In emergency survival, do what you must.

## Cleaning Game

- **Small animals**: Skin and gut before cooking
- **Practice**: Difficult but necessary skill
- **Cook thoroughly**: Parasites and bacteria common in wild game
