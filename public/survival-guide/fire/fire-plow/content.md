# Fire Plow

The fire plow is one of the oldest methods of creating fire through friction. It works by heating a wooden point against a groove in a softer board.

## What You Need

- **Fireboard**: A softwood board (cedar, willow, pine)
- **Plough**: A hard stick about 12 inches long
- **Tinder**: Dry grass, bark, or plant fibers

## Steps

1. **Prepare the fireboard**: Select a dry, soft piece of wood about 1 inch thick

2. **Create the groove**: Using the end of your plough or a hard stone, carve a straight groove from one edge toward the center

3. **Widen the groove**: The groove should be about 1/4 inch wide and deep enough to hold the plough tip

4. **Create a depression**: At the end of the groove, create a small circular depression where the heat will build up

5. **Prepare the plough**: Shape the end into a blunt point

6. **Position**: Kneel behind the fireboard, bracing it with your foot or against your leg

7. **Begin plowing**: Place the plough tip in the depression

8. **Push and pull**: Using both hands, push the plough forward and pull it back rapidly

9. **Generate heat**: Continue the motion consistently until smoke appears

10. **Create an ember**: A small black ember will form in the depression

11. **Transfer**: Carefully move the ember to your tinder bundle

12. **Blow to flame**: Gently blow on the ember until it ignites

## Tips

- The motion should be long and rhythmic
- Consistent speed is more important than speed
- The dust created is your ember - don't lose it
- Softwoods like cedar and willow work best
- This technique requires practice to master

## Troubleshooting

- **No smoke**: You're not generating enough friction - increase speed and pressure
- ** ember dies**: The wood is too wet or you're not transferring quickly enough
- **Wood burns**: You're pressing too hard - let the friction do the work
