# Fire Plow

The fire plow is one of the oldest methods of creating fire through friction. It works by heating a wooden point against a groove in a softer board.

## What You Need

- **Fireboard**: A softwood board (cedar, willow, pine)
- **Plough**: A hard stick about 12 inches long
- **Tinder**: Dry grass, bark, or plant fibers

:::tip
Softwoods like cedar and willow work best - they're easier to grind and create friction with.
:::

## Steps

1. **Prepare the fireboard**
   - Select a dry, soft piece of wood about 1 inch thick

2. **Create the groove**
   - Using the end of your plough or a hard stone
   - Carve a straight groove from one edge toward the center

3. **Widen the groove**
   - The groove should be about 1/4 inch wide
   - Deep enough to hold the plough tip

4. **Create a depression**
   - At the end of the groove, create a small circular depression
   - This is where the heat will build up

5. **Prepare the plough**
   - Shape the end into a blunt point

6. **Position yourself**
   - Kneel behind the fireboard
   - Brace it with your foot or against your leg

7. **Begin plowing**
   - Place the plough tip in the depression

8. **Push and pull**
   - Using both hands, push the plough forward and pull it back rapidly

9. **Generate heat**
   - Continue the motion consistently
   - Smoke will appear, then an ember

10. **Transfer**
    - Carefully move the ember to your tinder bundle

11. **Blow to flame**
    - Gently blow on the ember until it ignites

:::warning
The motion should be long and rhythmic - consistent speed is more important than speed. Let the friction do the work, don't press too hard.
:::

## Troubleshooting

- **No smoke?** You're not generating enough friction - increase speed and pressure
- **Ember dies?** The wood is too wet or you're not transferring quickly enough
- **Wood burns?** You're pressing too hard - relax and let friction work
