# Flint and Steel Fire Starting

Flint and steel creates sparks that ignite tinder. This method works reliably in any weather condition and has been used for thousands of years.

## What You Need

- **Flint**: Any hard stone that can produce sparks (flint, quartz, chert)
- **Steel**: A carbon steel striker or the back of a knife
- **Char cloth**: Charred cotton or linen cloth (optional but highly recommended)
- **Tinder**: Dry grass, bark, cattail fluff, or dryer lint

## Steps

1. **Prepare your tinder**: Gather a handful of fine, dry tinder. Fluff it up and create a bird's nest shape

2. **Position the flint**: Hold the flint in your non-dominant hand at a 45-degree angle

3. **Place the steel**: Hold the steel striker at the edge of the flint, angled away from you

4. **Strike**: Quickly scrape the steel down across the flint in a motion away from your body

5. **Catch the spark**: A spark should land on your tinder or char cloth

6. **Transfer to tinder**: If using char cloth, place it in your tinder nest

7. **Nurture the spark**: Gently blow on the spark until it glows and ignites

8. **Build the fire**: Once you have a small flame, carefully add kindling

## Tips

- The steel must be high-carbon steel - stainless steel won't produce sparks
- Strike at an angle - about 20-30 degrees works best
- Hold tinder close to where you're striking
- Create a spark pocket with char cloth for best results
- Practice the striking motion - it needs to be quick and confident

## Making Char Cloth

1. Cut cotton or linen into small squares
2. Place in a container with a small hole (tin can with nail hole)
3. Heat until smoke stops coming out
4. The cloth will be black but not burned - it's now highly flammable
