# Water-Proof Fire

Starting a fire in wet conditions requires extra preparation and techniques. Here's how to get a fire going even in rain.

## Finding Dry Materials

1. **Look under overhangs**: Rocks, fallen trees, cliff faces
2. **Check dead trees**: Standing dead wood is often dry inside
3. **Split wood**: The inside of logs is often dry
4. **Collect bark**: Birch and cedar bark repels water
5. **Dig for dry wood**: Under leaf litter, wood may be dry

:::tip
Focus on finding dry tinder and kindling - fuel wood can be damp.
:::

## Preparation

1. **Create a dry bed**: Clear wet ground, use logs or rocks
2. **Build a roof**: Use logs, branches, or improvise
3. **Gather extra tinder**: You'll need more than usual
4. **Prepare feather sticks**: For catching spark

## Techniques

### The Dakota Fire Hole

1. Dig a hole about 1 foot deep
2. Dig a small air tunnel to the hole
3. Build fire in the hole
4. Hole shields fire from wind and rain

### The Mound Fire

1. Find a flat rock or log
2. Build fire on top of it
3. Keeps fire above wet ground
4. Rock also reflects heat

### Using a Fire Reflector

1. Build a wall of logs behind fire
2. Reflects heat and light forward
3. Creates a sheltered cooking area

## Keeping Fire Alive

- [ ] Start with smaller wood than usual
- [ ] Add fuel gradually as fire establishes
- [ ] Keep dry tinder in reserve
- [ ] Protect from rain with improvised roof
