# Navigation by Stars

At night, stars can guide you reliably. The North Star (Polaris) is the most important navigation star in the Northern Hemisphere.

## Finding the North Star (Northern Hemisphere)

### Big Dipper Method

1. **Find the Big Dipper**: Distinctive ladle shape, part of Ursa Major
2. **Follow the pointer stars**: The two stars at the front of the "cup"
3. **Draw a line**: About 5x the distance between these stars
4. **That's Polaris**: The North Star, located at the end of the Little Dipper handle

### Cassiopeia Method

1. **Find Cassiopeia**: W or M shaped constellation
2. **It's opposite the Big Dipper**: On the other side of Polaris
3. **Bisect the W**: Draw line through middle of W, extend outward
4. **This points toward Polaris**

## Using Polaris

- Polaris is within 1 degree of true north
- Once found, face it and you're facing north
- Extend to south by turning 180 degrees

## Southern Hemisphere (Southern Cross)

1. **Find the Southern Cross**: Small cross constellation
2. **Extend the long axis**: About 4.5 times the length of the cross
3. **That's south**: The point where this line ends is due south
4. **Find south on horizon**: Drop straight down to the horizon

### False Cross

- The Southern Cross can be confused with the False Cross
- The False Cross is larger and dimmer
- The Southern Cross has brighter stars and a "pointer" star nearby (Alpha Centauri)

## Other Star Navigation

### East-West Finding

- **Stars rise in east, set in west** (with exceptions near poles)
- Watch stars for a short time to determine direction
- Less accurate than using fixed points

### Constellations for Practice

- **Orion**: Hunter constellation, visible worldwide
- **Cassiopeia**: W-shaped, circumpolar in north
- **Leo**: Lion, prominent spring constellation
- **Scorpius**: Scorpion, prominent summer constellation

## Tips

- Practice finding constellations in good weather before needed
- Allow your eyes to adjust to darkness (20+ minutes)
- Red light preserves night vision if you have a light
- Use horizon as backup if stars are unclear
- Always confirm with another method if possible
