# Tracking Distance with Pacing

Knowing what direction you are going is only half of navigation. The other half is knowing exactly how far you have traveled. Pacing is the most reliable method for tracking distance on foot.

## Finding Your Pace Count

A "pace" is defined as every time your **left foot** strikes the ground.

1. **Measure 100 meters** on a flat, known terrain (like a track or football field).
2. Start walking with your right foot.
3. Every time your left foot hits the ground, count "1... 2... 3...".
4. The number of left-foot strikes it takes to cross 100 meters is your **Pace Count**.
5. *For the average adult, a 100-meter pace count is usually between 60 and 70 paces.*

## Using Ranger Beads (Pace Counters)

Keeping the math in your head for hours is impossible. Ranger Beads are a simple manual abacus you can attach to your backpack to count hundreds of meters.

### Making Them
- Tie 9 beads into a bottom section of cord.
- Tie a knot above them.
- Tie 4 beads into the top section of the same cord.

### How to Count
1. Start walking. Every time you count your personalized 100-meter pace count, pull **one bottom bead down**.
2. When you have pulled down all 9 bottom beads, you have walked 900 meters.
3. Walk your pace count one more time (1,000 meters / 1 Kilometer total).
4. Slide all 9 bottom beads back up, and pull **one top bead down**.
5. Each top bead represents 1 Kilometer. All 4 top beads = 4 Kilometers.

## Factors That Change Your Pace

Your pace count will not stay the same in the wilderness. You must adjust your count dynamically to stay accurate:

- **Slopes**: Uphill shrinks your stride (more paces per 100m). Downhill extends it (fewer paces).
- **Fatigue**: Being tired shrinks your stride.
- **Heavy Loads**: Carrying a heavy backpack shrinks your stride.
- **Vegetation**: Pushing through thick brush slows you down and requires more steps per 100m.

:::tip
Always round your pacing estimates. In thick brush or uphill, add 10-20% more paces to your base 100m count to remain accurate.
:::
