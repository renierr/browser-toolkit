# Evacuation Route Planning

In fast-moving emergencies (wildfire, flood, storm, chemical incident), a preplanned route can save critical minutes.

## Build a 3-Route Plan

Prepare three options from home, workplace, or camp:

1. **Primary route** - fastest normal path
2. **Secondary route** - avoids main bottlenecks
3. **Last-resort route** - on foot if roads fail

## Route Selection Rules

- Prefer roads less likely to flood or be blocked by landslides
- Avoid narrow canyons in storm conditions
- Identify bridges and known choke points in advance
- Mark safe stop locations every 30-60 minutes of travel

## Go/No-Go Triggers

Decide in advance when to leave:

- Official evacuation order issued
- Fire/smoke visible within defined distance
- River level rises above local warning threshold
- Communications fail while hazard risk is increasing

:::warning
Leaving too late is one of the most common fatal errors in large-scale disasters.
:::

## Family or Team Coordination

- [ ] Shared contact list on paper and in phone
- [ ] One out-of-area check-in contact
- [ ] Rendezvous point A and backup point B
- [ ] Clear rule for regrouping if separated

## Final 60-Second Departure Check

- [ ] Water, medications, IDs, and power bank packed
- [ ] Weather-appropriate clothing on
- [ ] Utilities handled if time allows
- [ ] One last status message sent
- [ ] Route chosen and everyone informed

:::tip
Practice your evacuation route at least once every 6 months and update it after major road or area changes.
:::
