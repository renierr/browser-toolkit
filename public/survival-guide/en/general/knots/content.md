# Essential Survival Knots

Knowing a few reliable knots is much better than knowing many useless ones. These three knots cover 90% of survival needs.

## 1. Bowline (The Rescue Knot)

The bowline creates a fixed loop at the end of a rope that will not slip or tighten under load.

**Uses**: Rescues, securing lines to trees, creating a loop for a pulley system.

**Steps:**
1. Form a small loop in the rope (the "rabbit hole")
2. Pass the free end (the "rabbit") up through the hole
3. Run it around the standing line (the "tree")
4. Pass it back down through the hole
5. Pull both the free end and the standing line to tighten

:::tip
Rhyme: "The rabbit comes out of the hole, goes around the tree, and back down the hole."
:::

## 2. Taut-Line Hitch (The Tent Knot)

An adjustable loop knot for use on lines under tension. It grips when under tension but can be slid when tension is removed.

**Uses**: Tying tent guy lines, securing loads on vehicles, adjustable temporary moorings.

**Steps:**
1. Pass the working end around an anchor object
2. Wrap the working end around the standing part twice, working back toward the anchor
3. Make one more wrap around the standing part on the *outside* of the first two wraps
4. Tuck the end through the new loop made
5. Pull tight

## 3. Square Knot (Reef Knot)

Used to secure a rope or line around an object. It should never be used to tie two ropes together if they will be under heavy load.

**Uses**: Tying bandages, securing bundles, tying shoelaces.

**Steps:**
1. Right over Left, and pull under.
2. Left over Right, and pull under.

:::warning
The square knot is NOT safe for joining two ropes that will hold human weight. It can slip and capsize!
:::
