# Mental Survival (S.T.O.P.)

Survival is 10% physical skill and 90% psychology. 
When people realize they are lost or in grave danger, the body dumps immense adrenaline, inciting panic. Panic causes you to run blindly, sweat profusely, damage your gear, and leave trails leading straight to your death.

If you realize you are in trouble, firmly institute the **S.T.O.P.** principle immediately:

## S = Sit Down
Stop moving immediately. Take the pack off. Sit on a log or a rock. Force your body to rest. Take deep, slow breaths. Doing this immediately short-circuits the brain's "flee" response. Do not move from that spot until your heart rate is normal.

## T = Think
Do you know broadly where you are? What time of day is it? How did you get here? What are your most pressing needs in order of priority (usually Medical -> Shelter -> Fire -> Water).

## O = Observe
Look around you without moving.
- Is there an immediate hazard nearby?
- What resources do you have? 
- What resources are provided by the environment right now (pine bows for shelter, dry logs for fire)?
- Observe the weather conditions.

## P = Plan
Formulate a deliberate, step-by-step plan based on your observations. 
If it is getting dark in 2 hours, your plan is NOT to blindly hike out—your plan is to immediately build a shelter and fire where you stand.

:::tip
Maintaining a Positive Mental Attitude (PMA) is the strongest indicator of survival. Will to live overcomes severe physical limitations. Expect things to be hard, but absolutely refuse to give up.
:::

## The Rule of 5 "P"s
**P**roper **P**rior **P**lanning **P**revents **P**oor **P**erformance.

## Checklist
- [ ] Stop walking the exact second you realize you are lost
- [ ] Force yourself to physically sit down and drink water
- [ ] Stabilize your breathing 
- [ ] Honestly assess remaining daylight and weather
- [ ] Make a solid plan before taking another physical step
