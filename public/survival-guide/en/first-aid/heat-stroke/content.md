# Heat Stroke

Heat stroke is a life-threatening emergency. It occurs when body temperature rises above 104°F (40°C). The body can no longer cool itself.

## Signs of Heat Stroke

- High body temperature (103°F / 39°C or higher)
- Hot, red, dry, or damp skin
- Rapid, strong pulse
- Confusion
- Headache
- Nausea
- Loss of consciousness

:::error
This is a medical emergency. Cool the person immediately and seek help.
:::

## Immediate Actions

1. **Move to cool place** - Shade or air conditioning
2. **Cool rapidly** - Use whatever means available
3. **Call for help** - This is life-threatening

## Cooling Methods

### Ice Packs (if available)

- Place ice packs on neck, armpits, groin
- Wrap in cloth - don't apply directly to skin

### Water

- Wet sheets and place on person
- Fan while wetting
- Immerse in cool water if possible

### Evaporation Cooling

- Wet person's skin
- Fan continuously
- Works well in dry climates

### Ice/Cold Water Immersion

- Best method if available
- Put person in cold water
- Or wrap in cold, wet sheets

:::warning
Do NOT give fluids - person may be unconscious or vomiting. Focus on cooling first.
:::

## While Waiting for Help

- [ ] Move to shade/cool area
- [ ] Remove excess clothing
- [ ] Apply cool water to skin
- [ ] Fan the person
- [ ] Place ice packs on neck, armpits, groin
- [ ] Monitor breathing - be ready for CPR

## Prevention

- [ ] Stay hydrated
- [ ] Take breaks in shade
- [ ] Wear light, loose clothing
- [ ] Avoid peak sun hours
- [ ] Acclimate slowly to heat
