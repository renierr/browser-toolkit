# Snake Bites

Snake bites can be life-threatening. Knowing how to respond can save a life.

## Types of Snakes

### Venomous (in North America)

- Rattlesnakes
- Copperheads
- Cottonmouths (water moccasins)
- Coral snakes

### Signs of Venomous Bite

- Puncture wounds (may be 1 or 2)
- Severe pain and swelling
- Nausea and vomiting
- Difficulty breathing
- Blurred vision
- Rapid heart rate

## Immediate Actions

1. **Move away** from the snake - don't try to catch or kill it
2. **Keep calm** - panic increases heart rate, spreading venom faster
3. **Remove jewelry** - rings, watches, tight clothing before swelling
4. **Position** - keep bite below heart level
5. **Clean wound** - gently with water

:::warning
Do NOT cut the wound. Do NOT suck out venom. Do NOT apply ice. These do not help and can cause more harm.
:::

## What NOT To Do

- [ ] Do not try to catch the snake
- [ ] Do not cut the wound
- [ ] Do not suck out venom
- [ ] Do not apply ice or tourniquet
- [ ] Do not give alcohol
- [ ] Do not raise wound above heart

## Monitoring

- [ ] Note time of bite
- [ ] Watch for swelling spreading
- [ ] Monitor breathing
- [ ] Watch for nausea/vomiting
- [ ] Note any allergic reactions

## Getting Help

- Seek medical attention as soon as possible
- Try to describe the snake (color, pattern, shape of head)
- Keep person still and calm
- Be prepared to perform CPR if needed

:::tip
Most snakes are not venomous. Even venomous bites don't always inject venom - dry bites happen.
:::
