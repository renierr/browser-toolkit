# Recognizing and Treating Dehydration

Dehydration can become life-threatening quickly, especially in heat, high altitude, or during heavy exertion.

## Early Warning Signs

- Dry mouth and strong thirst
- Dark yellow urine or very little urine
- Headache and dizziness
- Fatigue or irritability
- Muscle cramps

## Severe Danger Signs

- No sweating despite heat
- Confusion or poor decision-making
- Rapid pulse and breathing
- Fainting
- Very dry skin and lips

:::error
If the person is confused, fainting, vomiting repeatedly, or unable to drink, seek emergency help immediately.
:::

## Immediate Field Treatment

1. **Move to shade**
   - Stop activity immediately
   - Loosen tight clothing
2. **Cool the body**
   - Fan the person
   - Place wet cloth on neck, armpits, and groin
3. **Rehydrate slowly**
   - Give small, repeated sips every 2-3 minutes
   - Use clean water or oral rehydration solution
4. **Monitor for worsening**
   - Check mental status every few minutes
   - Stop giving fluids if vomiting continues

## Improvised Oral Rehydration Mix

For 1 liter of safe water:

- 6 level teaspoons sugar
- 1/2 level teaspoon salt

Stir until fully dissolved.

:::warning
Too much salt can make dehydration worse. Measure carefully.
:::

## Prevention Checklist

- [ ] Drink before thirst becomes strong
- [ ] Rest in shade during peak heat
- [ ] Pace activity and avoid overexertion
- [ ] Replace both water and salts
- [ ] Track urine color and frequency
