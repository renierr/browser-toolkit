# Treating Frostbite

Frostbite occurs when skin and underlying tissues freeze. It primarily affects the extremities: fingers, toes, nose, ears, and cheeks. Treating it incorrectly can shatter the frozen cell walls, leading to amputation.

## Recognizing Frostbite

Frostbite progresses in stages:
1. **Frostnip**: Skin turns pale or red and feels very cold. It feels numb or prickly. No permanent damage.
2. **Superficial Frostbite**: Skin turns white or grayish-yellow and feels unusually firm or waxy to the touch. Blisters may form later.
3. **Deep Frostbite**: Skin goes completely white, bluish, or mottled. The tissues underneath feel completely hard and wooden. The area is completely numb and pain-free.

## Proper Rewarming Procedure

1. **Get to Shelter**: Move the victim out of the cold and wind. Remove any wet clothing.
2. **Gentle Rewarming**: 
   - For mild frostnip, use skin-to-skin contact (put cold fingers in your armpits or hold cold feet against a partner's warm stomach).
   - For severe frostbite, soak the affected area in **warm water—NOT hot.** The water should feel comfortable to the touch for unaffected skin (about 100°F to 105°F / 38°C to 40°C). 
   - Keep the water circulating.
3. **Pain Management**: Rewarming is excruciatingly painful. Provide painkillers if available.

## What NOT to Do (Critical)

:::error
NEVER rub or massage frostbitten skin. The tissues contain microscopic ice crystals. Rubbing them acts like sandpaper, shredding the cells from the inside out and guaranteeing tissue death.
:::

- **Do NOT use dry heat**: Do not place frostbitten hands near a roaring fire, radiator, or heating pad. The numb skin cannot feel the extreme heat and will suffer severe third-degree burns before the tissue thaws.
- **Do NOT rewarm if refreezing is possible**: If you are still miles out in the wilderness, it is better to walk on solidly frozen toes than to thaw them out and allow them to refreeze later. Thawing and refreezing causes massive, irreversible gangrene.
- **Do NOT pop blisters**: Frostbite blisters protect the underlying healing skin from infection.
