# Applying a Tourniquet

A tourniquet is a life-saving device used to control massive, life-threatening arterial bleeding from a limb (arm or leg).

## When to Use a Tourniquet

- Bright red, spurting, or pulsating bleeding
- Bleeding that pools rapidly on the ground
- Bleeding that cannot be stopped by direct pressure
- Amputations or near-amputations

:::warning
Only use a tourniquet on limbs (arms and legs). Never place a tourniquet around the neck, torso, or joints.
:::

## How to Apply a Tourniquet (Commercial: CAT / SOF-T)

1. **Position**: Place the tourniquet high and tight on the extremity, strictly *above* the bleeding site. (2-3 inches above the wound, or "high and tight" if the wound source is unclear).
2. **Pull Tight**: Pull the strap as tight as possible before twisting.
3. **Twist the Windlass**: Turn the rod (windlass) until the bleeding completely stops.
4. **Secure the Rod**: Lock the rod into the clip.
5. **Note the Time**: Write the time of application on the tourniquet or the patient's forehead (e.g., "TQ 14:30").

## How to Improvise a Tourniquet

If you don't have a commercial tourniquet, you can improvise one, though they are less effective.

### Materials
- **Strap**: A triangular bandage, scarf, very thick rope, or sturdy strip of cloth (at least 1.5 to 2 inches wide. Do not use thin wire or shoelaces, as they will cut through tissue).
- **Windlass**: A sturdy stick, metal rod, carabiner, or flashlight.

### Steps
1. Wrap the cloth around the limb 2-3 inches above the wound.
2. Tie a standard square knot.
3. Place your stick (windlass) on top of the knot, and tie a second knot over the stick to secure it.
4. Twist the stick rapidly until the bleeding stops.
5. Use a second piece of cloth to tie the stick in place so it doesn't unwind.
6. Note the time of application.

## Critical Rules

- [ ] It WILL hurt. A properly applied tourniquet is extremely painful. Do not loosen it because the patient complains of pain.
- [ ] NEVER loosen or remove a tourniquet once applied. Only medical professionals should remove it.
- [ ] Do not cover the tourniquet with clothing; responders need to see it immediately.
