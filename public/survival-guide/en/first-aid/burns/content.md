# Treating Burns

Burns are tissue damage caused by heat, chemicals, electricity, or sunlight. Proper treatment can prevent infection and minimize scarring.

## Burn Classification

### First Degree (Minor)

- Redness
- Pain
- Minor swelling
- Example: sunburn, brief contact with hot object

### Second Degree (Moderate)

- Blisters
- Severe pain
- Swelling
- Wet or weepy appearance

### Third Degree (Severe)

- White or charred skin
- May be painless (nerve damage)
- Requires immediate professional care

## Treatment Steps

### First Degree Burns

1. **Cool the burn** - Hold under cool (not cold) running water for 10-20 minutes
2. **Remove constrictions** - Remove rings, watches, tight clothing before swelling
3. **Protect** - Apply clean, non-stick bandage if needed
4. **Pain relief** - Over-the-counter pain medication if available
5. **Don't break blisters** - This increases infection risk

### Second Degree Burns

1. **Cool immediately** - Same as first degree
2. **Don't break blisters** - Even if large
3. **Cover loosely** - With sterile, non-stick bandage
4. **Elevate** - If possible, raise burned area above heart
5. **Watch for infection** - Increased pain, redness, or fever
6. **Seek medical help** - As soon as possible

### Third Degree Burns

1. **Call for emergency help** immediately
2. **Don't remove** clothing stuck to burn
3. **Cover loosely** - With clean, dry cloth or bandage
4. **Check breathing** - Be prepared to perform CPR
5. **Position** - Keep victim lying down, elevate feet if no spinal injury
6. **Monitor for shock** - See shock guide
7. **Keep warm** - Burns cause body temperature to drop

:::warning
Never apply ice directly to burns. Never use butter, oil, or grease on burns.
:::

## When to Seek Help

- [ ] Burns larger than your palm
- [ ] Burns on face, hands, feet, groin, or joints
- [ ] All third-degree burns
- [ ] Chemical or electrical burns
- [ ] Signs of infection
