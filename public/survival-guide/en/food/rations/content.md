# Managing Food Rations

While humans can survive roughly 3 weeks without food, physical and mental performance degrades rapidly after just 3 days. Managing whatever food you have is critical.

## Caloric Budgeting

In intense survival situations (moving, building shelters, stress), you may burn upwards of 4,000 to 5,000 calories a day.
If your supply is limited, **rationing from day one is essential.**

1. **Take Inventory**: Empty all pockets and bags. Count every calorie you have access to. 
2. **Estimate Rescue Time**: Honestly assess how many days until you expect to be found or reach civilization.
3. **Daily Allowance**: Divide your total calories by the expected days.

## Eating Strategically

- **Don't eat on day one**: The adrenaline of a fresh emergency suppresses hunger. Skip eating for the first 24 hours to stretch rations.
- **Consume fat first, protein last**: Fats provide the densest energy. Proteins require significant water to metabolize properly. If water is extremely short, *do not eat protein or salt*.
- **Small meals**: Eat small bites throughout the day to keep blood sugar stable.

## The Rule of Threes (And Food)
- 3 minutes without oxygen
- 3 hours without shelter in harsh environments
- 3 days without water
- **3 weeks without food**

:::warning
Never consume plants or wild game if you aren't 100% positive of their identification, and do not waste calories chasing small animals that burn more energy to catch than they provide.
:::

## Maintaining Morale

Ration fatigue is a real psychological danger. Saving one small piece of candy or high-value food item for an especially difficult day can provide a life-saving mental morale boost.

## Checklist
- [ ] Inventory all available food immediately
- [ ] Don't eat in the first 24 hours of an emergency
- [ ] Calculate a daily carbohydrate and fat budget
- [ ] Do not eat heavy proteins if water is severely limited
