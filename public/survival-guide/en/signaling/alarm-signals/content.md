# Alarm Siren Signals

Understanding alarm siren patterns can help you respond appropriately during emergencies. Different patterns have different meanings.

## International Maritime Distress Signals

These are internationally recognized:

| Pattern           | Morse | Meaning                   |
| ----------------- | ----- | ------------------------- |
| Short blast       | O     | "O" - Man overboard       |
| Two short blasts  | OS    | "OS" - Stop immediately   |
| One long blast    | A     | "A" - I'm heading to port |
| Two long blasts   | AA    | "AA" - Coming into port   |
| Three long blasts | SSS   | Danger, I'm going astern  |

:::tip
When in doubt, tune to VHF Channel 16 (156.8 MHz) - the international distress frequency.
:::

## Air Raid / Civil Defense Sirens

### Standard Patterns

**Rising-Falling Tone (Wail)**

- Used for: Air raid warning, missile attack
- Action: Seek shelter immediately

**Steady Tone (Siren)**

- Used for: All clear, danger passed
- Action: Return to normal activities

**Pulsing/Alternating**

- Used for: Chemical/biological hazard
- Action: Shelter in place, seal windows

### Country-Specific Patterns

#### Germany (Warnsystem)

- **Warnung** (Warning): 3-minute steady tone
- **Alarm** (Alarm): Fast oscillating tone
- **Entwarnung** (All clear): 3-minute steady tone

#### United Kingdom

- **Nuclear alert**: Three short bursts, pause, repeat
- **Flood warning**: Alternating high-low tones

#### Netherlands

- **Open Structure** (test): 1 minute continuous tone (first Monday of month at noon)

#### France

- **Danger majeure**: Continuous 3-minute tone
- **Test**: Usually announced in advance

## US Emergency Alerts

### Weather Alerts

**Tornado Warning**

- Steady tone (varies by county)
- Action: Take immediate shelter

**Severe Thunderstorm**

- Short pulsing tones
- Action: Seek shelter, avoid windows

**Fire Alarm**

- Pulsing pattern
- Action: Evacuate building

### Test Schedules

- **Severe Weather Test**: Typically first Wednesday of month at 10am local time
- **Civil Defense Test**: Often at noon on first Saturday

:::warning
Actual emergencies override test schedules. When in doubt, treat the alert as real.
:::

## DIY / Improvised Siren Signals

If you need to signal without a siren:

### Whistle Codes

- **One blast**: "Yes, I'm here"
- **Two blasts**: "No"
- **Three blasts**: "HELP - emergency"
- **Long continuous**: "Warning - danger approaching"

### Vehicle Horn Patterns

- **3 short honks**: Greeting (rural areas)
- **Long continuous**: Emergency distress
- **Honk-honk-pause-honk**: Signal to regroup

### Getting Attention

- Mirror flashes (day)
- Flashlight at night
- Fire signals
- Ground-to-air signals
