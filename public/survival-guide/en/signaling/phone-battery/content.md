# Emergency Phone Battery Strategy

A phone can be your most powerful rescue tool, but only if you manage battery life aggressively.

## First 5 Minutes

1. **Send your location**
   - Text exact location, direction of travel, injuries, and supplies
   - Send to trusted contacts and emergency services where available
2. **Enable low-power setup**
   - Turn on battery saver mode
   - Reduce brightness to minimum readable level
   - Disable Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, and background app refresh
3. **Preserve signal attempts**
   - Keep airplane mode on when no signal is available
   - Check for signal in short scheduled windows

## Messaging Priority

Use short structured messages:

- Who you are
- Where you are (landmarks, coordinates, trail markers)
- Condition (injuries, weather, shelter)
- Plan (staying put or moving)

:::tip
Text messages often send with weaker signal than voice calls and use less battery.
:::

## Charging and Temperature

- Keep phone warm in cold weather (inside inner clothing)
- Keep phone shaded in hot weather
- Use a power bank only when needed, not continuously
- Charge to around 80-90% to reduce wasted conversion losses in repeated top-ups

## Signal Search Routine

- [ ] Every 30-60 minutes, leave airplane mode for 2-3 minutes
- [ ] Move to higher ground if safe
- [ ] Avoid steep gullies and dense tree cover
- [ ] Stop moving once contact is made unless instructed

:::warning
Do not waste battery on videos, photos, or non-essential apps during an emergency.
:::

## If Battery Is Critically Low

- Send one final complete status text
- Enable airplane mode
- Set alarm intervals to recheck signal
- Keep device off between check windows if needed
