# Banking a Fire

Starting a fire from scratch every morning is time-consuming and depletes your tinder resources. "Banking" a fire allows you to preserve the glowing coals overnight so you can instantly blow them back to life the next morning.

## The Principle

Banking works by deeply burying hot coals in an insulating layer of ash and soil. This starves the fire of oxygen, slowing the burn rate to an absolute crawl, while the ash acts as a blanket that traps the extreme heat inside.

## How to Bank a Fire

1. **Build a Coal Bed**: Before going to sleep, let the flames die down until you have a thick, glowing bed of solid hardwood coals. Avoid banking soft woods (like pine), as they burn to dust too quickly.
2. **Compact the Coals**: Use a stick to rake all the hot coals together into a tight mound in the center of the fire pit.
3. **Cover with Ash**: Using an improvised shovel (bark or a flat rock), meticulously cover the mound of coals entirely with white/gray powder ash from the fire pit. Make this layer thick (at least 2 inches).
4. **Seal with Soil**: Optional but recommended—add a thin top layer of dry dirt over the ash to further restrict oxygen and insulate the bed. Do not use wet mud.

:::warning
Do not completely smother the fire by pressing down hard with heavy dirt. The coals need a microscopic amount of oxygen to stay alive. You are insulating them, not suffocating them.
:::

## Reviving the Fire in the Morning

1. Carefully scrape away the dirt and ash layers. Underneath, a dark lump may appear.
2. Poke the lump—it should break apart to reveal bright red, burning embers.
3. Rake the embers together.
4. Place highly combustible tinder (like dry leaves or a feather stick) directly onto the embers.
5. Blow gently and steadily into the base of the embers until the tinder catches flame.
6. Feed the flame with small twigs, building back up to a full fire.
