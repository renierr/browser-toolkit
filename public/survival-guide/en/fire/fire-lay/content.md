# Fire Lay

The fire lay is how you arrange your firewood. A good fire lay ensures proper airflow, steady burning, and easy flame propagation.

![Fire lay diagram](./survival-guide/images/fire/fire-lay/diagram.svg)

## The Tepee Fire Lay

Best for: Starting fires, wet conditions

1. Place tinder in center
2. Arrange kindling in tepee shape over tinder
3. Leave gap for air flow
4. Light from inside

## The Log Cabin Fire Lay

Best for: Long-lasting fires, cooking

1. Place two parallel logs as base
2. Add two more across (like Lincoln logs)
3. Continue building up with smaller wood
4. Creates sturdy structure with air pockets

## The Star Fire Lay

Best for: Long burns, overnight fires

1. Build fire in center
2. Point logs inward like spokes of a wheel
3. Push logs inward as they burn
4. Keeps fire contained, burns slowly

## The Windproof Fire Lay

Best for: windy conditions

1. Dig shallow hole or trench
2. Place larger logs as windbreak on windward side
3. Build fire in shelter of hole
4. Stack smaller wood on windward side

:::tip
The key principle: Air must be able to flow through the fire. Don't pack wood too tightly.
:::

## Fire Lay Layers

1. **Tinder**: Fine, dry material to catch spark
2. **Kindling**: Small sticks (finger-width) to feed flame
3. **Fuel wood**: Larger logs to sustain fire

:::warning
Always build fires in safe locations away from dry vegetation, overhanging branches, and flammable materials.
:::
