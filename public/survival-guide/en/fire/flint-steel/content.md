# Flint and Steel Fire Starting

Flint and steel creates sparks that ignite tinder. This reliable method works in any weather condition and has been used for thousands of years.

## What You Need

- **Flint**: Any hard stone that can produce sparks (flint, quartz, chert)
- **Steel**: A carbon steel striker or the back of a knife
- **Char cloth**: Charred cotton or linen cloth (highly recommended)
- **Tinder**: Dry grass, bark, cattail fluff, or dryer lint

:::tip
Char cloth is the key to success - it catches sparks easily and transfers them to your tinder bundle.
:::

## Steps

1. **Prepare your tinder**
   - Gather a handful of fine, dry tinder
   - Fluff it up and create a bird's nest shape

2. **Position the flint**
   - Hold the flint in your non-dominant hand at a 45-degree angle

3. **Place the steel**
   - Hold the steel striker at the edge of the flint
   - Angle it away from your body

4. **Strike**
   - Quickly scrape the steel down across the flint
   - Motion should be away from your body

5. **Catch the spark**
   - A spark should land on your tinder or char cloth

6. **Transfer to tinder**
   - If using char cloth, place it in your tinder nest

7. **Nurture the spark**
   - Gently blow on the spark until it glows

8. **Build the fire**
   - Once you have a small flame, carefully add kindling

:::warning
The steel must be high-carbon steel - stainless steel won't produce sparks. Test your striker before relying on it.
:::

## Making Char Cloth

1. Cut cotton or linen into small squares
2. Place in a container with a small hole (tin can with nail hole)
3. Heat until smoke stops coming out
4. The cloth will be black but not burned

:::tip
Keep char cloth in a waterproof container - it works even when damp.
:::
