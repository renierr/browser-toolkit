# Tarp Shelter Configurations

A simple specialized tarp (or ponchos, or heavy plastic sheeting) combined with paracord is the ultimate lightweight survival shelter.

## 1. The Classic A-Frame

Best for: High winds, rain protection, sleeping two people.

1. String a ridgeline (paracord) tight between two trees at waist height.
2. Throw the tarp over the ridgeline perfectly in the middle.
3. Stake out the four corners to the ground.
4. If your tarp has center tie-outs along the edges, pull them tight to create more interior volume.

**Pros:** Great weather protection, sheds water quickly.
**Cons:** Minimal headroom, only open at two ends.

## 2. The Plow Point (Diamond)

Best for: Deflecting wind, single person sleeping, very fast setup.

1. Stake down three corners of the tarp directly to the ground to form a triangle base.
2. Take the remaining fourth corner and tie it up to a tree, a hiking pole, or a sturdy branch.
3. Pull the suspended corner taut.

**Pros:** Extremely fast, provides a low profile against wind, heat generated by you reflects back efficiently.
**Cons:** Vulnerable if wind changes direction to face the open side.

## 3. The Lean-To

Best for: Sitting by a fire, maximum ventilation, mild weather.

1. String a ridgeline between two trees, slightly higher than your head.
2. Attach one side of the tarp to the ridgeline.
3. Pull the opposite side to the ground at a 45-degree angle against the wind and stake it down.

:::tip
Build your fire directly in front of the open side of the Lean-To. The tarp will act as a massive reflector, bouncing the fire's radiant heat down onto you while you sleep.
:::

:::warning
Ensure your Lean-To is far enough away from the fire that flying embers won't melt holes in the nylon tarp!
:::

## Checklist
- [ ] Tie an incredibly strong ridgeline (using a taut-line hitch)
- [ ] Angle the shelter so the closed side faces the prevailing wind
- [ ] Stake all corners securely using branches or tent pegs
- [ ] Ensure the tarp material is completely taut to shed rain properly
