# Lean-to Shelter

A lean-to is a simple, quick-to-build shelter. It provides good protection from wind and rain when properly constructed.

## Choosing the Site

1. **Natural support**: Look for two trees about 7 feet apart
2. **Wind protection**: Build with the open side away from prevailing wind
3. **Level ground**: Find or create a level sleeping area
4. **Resources**: Near wood, water, and materials for cover

## Materials Needed

- **Ridgepole**: Long, strong pole (10-12 feet)
- **Support poles**: 6-8 poles, 4-5 feet long
- **Covering**: Branches, bark, pine boughs, leaves, tarp if available
- **Ground cover**: Pine boughs, leaves, grass

## Building Steps

### 1. Set the Ridgepole

1. Find two trees about 7 feet apart
2. Tie ridgepole between trees OR prop on forked stakes
3. Height: About 4 feet at front, lower at back
4. Should slope downward toward back

### 2. Add Support Poles

1. Lean poles against ridgepole on both sides
2. Space about 1 foot apart
3. Push bottom into ground to anchor
4. Creates the frame for covering

### 3. Add Horizontal Poles

1. Weave sticks horizontally through support poles
2. Creates lattice to hold covering
3. Works like a fence

### 4. Add Covering

1. Start from bottom, work up
2. Overlap like shingles
3. Use: pine boughs, branches, bark, leaves
4. Make layers thick - 6+ inches ideal
5. Cover all sides except entrance

### 5. Create Ground Bed

1. Fill with 6+ inches of pine boughs
2. Add leaves or grass if available
3. Creates insulation from cold ground

### 6. Build Fire Reflector

1. Build wall of logs or rocks opposite entrance
2. Reflects heat into shelter
3. Keep fire 6-8 feet away from cover

## Improvements

### Rain Protection

- Add a second layer at steeper angle on top
- Create a "roof" over the main shelter
- Use bark or larger branches as shingles

### Side Walls

- Add branches to sides for wind protection
- Make them removable for fire access

### Size Guidelines

- One person: 7 feet long x 4 feet wide
- Two people: 8 feet long x 7 feet wide
- Smaller is easier to heat

## Tips

- The lean-to is faster to build than debris hut
- Works best with a good fire
- Add more covering than you think needed
- Keep fire far enough to prevent fire
- Create a "door" that can be closed at night
