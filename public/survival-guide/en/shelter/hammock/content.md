# Hammock Safety & Setup

Hammocks are excellent survival shelters, especially in wet or tropical environments where the ground is soaked or covered in dangerous ground-dwelling wildlife. However, improperly hanging a hammock can be fatal.

## The Danger of "Widowmakers"

The biggest threat when sleeping in a hammock is not the hammock itself, but the trees you hang it from. A "Widowmaker" is a dead, detached, or hanging tree branch suspended high in the canopy above. A stiff wind in the middle of the night can knock these massive dead branches down directly onto your hammock.

### Checking for Widowmakers:
1. **Look Up:** Always thoroughly scan the canopy of your anchor trees and the space directly above your hammock.
2. **Shake the Tree:** If the trees are small enough, give the trunk a very hard shove. Look up to see if any dead wood rattles loose.
3. **Check the Soil:** Ensure the trees have solid root systems and aren't growing in completely saturated, muddy soil that might uproot under your weight.

## Rule of 30 Degrees

For maximum comfort, your suspension straps or paracord should angle from the tree down to the hammock at roughly a **30-degree angle**.
- If the straps are pulled too tight (horizontal), it places immense sheer force on the trees and the ropes, risking a snap.
- If they are hung too loose, you will sag aggressively backward like a banana, which damages spinal alignment.

## Drip Lines

If you are using a hammock in the rain, water will run down the tree trunk, travel along your suspension ropes, and soak directly into your hammock fabric, making you freeze.

**How to make Drip Lines:**
1. Tie a short, hanging string (like a shoelace or spare piece of paracord) tightly onto the suspension rope, positioned a few inches away from the hammock fabric.
2. When the rainwater travels down the main rope, it will hit the tied string, travel down it, and drip harmlessly to the ground before it can reach your bed.

## Staying Warm

A hammock exposes your underside to circulating cold air, drawing heat away much faster than sleeping on dirt (Convective heat loss). 
- [ ] You MUST place an insulating layer (like a sleeping pad, a thick wool blanket, or even a large pile of dry leaves packed inside your jacket) *underneath* your body inside the hammock.
