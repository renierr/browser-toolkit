# Lightning Safety

Lightning is a severe threat in the wilderness. If you hear thunder, lightning is close enough to strike you.

## The 30/30 Rule

1. **Count**: If the time between seeing a flash and hearing thunder is less than 30 seconds, the storm is close.
2. **Wait**: Stay in a safe location for 30 minutes after hearing the last rumble of thunder.

## Where to Avoid (Danger Zones)

- **Isolated tall objects**: Lone trees, flagpoles, or towers.
- **High elevation**: Ridges, peaks, or exposed hillsides.
- **Open areas**: Fields, meadows, or golf courses where YOU are the tallest object.
- **Water**: Lakes, rivers, and swimming pools.
- **Metal**: Wire fences, metal pipes, or rails.

## What To Do If Caught Out

1. **Descend immediately**: Get off peaks and ridges.
2. **Seek a depression**: Find a ravine or valley (but beware of flash floods).
3. **Find dense forest**: If in the woods, stay near lower stands of trees, avoiding the tallest ones.
4. **Assume the Lightning Position**:
   - Squat low to the ground to make yourself a smaller target.
   - Keep your feet together (minimizes step voltage).
   - Touch your heels together.
   - Cover your ears to protect from acoustic shock.
   - Keep your head tucked down.
   - **Do NOT lie flat on the ground.**

## Signs of an Imminent Strike

- Hair standing on end
- Skin tingling
- A faint buzzing or clicking sound
- Small metal objects vibrating

:::warning
If you experience these signs, drop immediately into the lightning position. A strike may be seconds away.
:::

## Group Safety

- [ ] Spread out (at least 50-100 feet apart) so one strike doesn't take out the whole group
- [ ] Drop metal frames/hiking poles
- [ ] Remove metal-framed backpacks
