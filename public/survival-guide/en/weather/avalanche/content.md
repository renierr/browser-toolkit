# Avalanche Survival

Avalanches are a deadly threat in snowy, mountainous terrain. Prevention and early recognition are the keys to survival.

## Identifying Avalanche Terrain

1. **Slope Angle**: Most avalanches occur on slopes between 30 and 45 degrees.
2. **Recent Weather**: Heavy snowfall, strong winds, or rapid warming significantly increase risk.
3. **Red Flags**:
   - Recent avalanche activity in the area
   - "Whumpfing" sounds or shooting cracks in the snow
   - Heavy rain on top of snow

## Essential Gear

If traveling in avalanche terrain, always carry the "Big Three":
- [ ] Avalanche Transceiver (Beacon)
- [ ] Collapsible Search Probe
- [ ] Sturdy Snow Shovel

## If Caught in an Avalanche

If the snow breaks around you:

1. **Yell!** Alert your partners immediately.
2. **Deploy airbag** (if you have one).
3. **Try to escape sideways**: Ski, snowboard, or run toward the flank (edge) of the slide.
4. **Discard heavy gear**: Drop poles and heavy packs that might drag you down (but keep your beacon!).
5. **Fight to stay on top**: "Swim" hard against the snow, thrashing your arms and legs to stay near the surface.

## As the Slide Slows Down

1. **Clear an airway**: Cupping your hands over your mouth and nose to create an air pocket.
2. **Thrust a hand up**: Try to push one arm toward the surface.
3. **Take a deep breath**: Expand your chest before the snow sets like concrete.
4. **Stay calm**: Conserve oxygen. Panic depletes your limited air supply much faster.

:::error
Once the snow stops moving, it sets almost instantly into a solid block of ice. You cannot dig yourself out. You must rely on your partners to rescue you.
:::
